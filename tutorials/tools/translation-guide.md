# International Collaboration & Translation Guide

At InkuA, we collaborate with people from all over the world. To ensure everyone understands each other, we use **English** as our common bridge language. This guide helps you translate your thoughts effectively before posting on GitHub or Discord.

## 1. Why Translate?

Even if your English isn't perfect, your ideas are valuable! Using translation tools allows you to:
*   **Understand** complex issues and requirements clearly.
*   **Express** your ideas precisely in your native language first.
*   **Collaborate** with team members who might not speak your language.

## 2. Recommended Tools

Here are the best free tools to help you translate and polish your writing:

*   **[DeepL Translator](https://www.deepl.com/translator)**: Often provides more natural-sounding translations than Google. Excellent for longer texts.
*   **[Google Translate](https://translate.google.com/)**: Great for quick words or translating entire webpages (using the browser extension).
*   **[Grammarly](https://www.grammarly.com/)**: Helps fix grammar and spelling mistakes in your English text.
*   **[ChatGPT](https://chat.openai.com/) / [Claude](https://claude.ai/)**: You can ask AI to "translate this to professional English" or "fix the grammar in this sentence".

## 3. Workflow: How to Comment and Publish

Follow these steps to communicate effectively on GitHub issues, Pull Requests, or documentation.

### Step 1: Understanding Others (Reading)
If you are unsure about a comment or issue description:
1.  Copy the English text.
2.  Paste it into **DeepL** or **Google Translate**.
3.  Translate it to your native language to ensure you grasp the nuances.

### Step 2: Drafting Your Response (Writing)
1.  **Draft in your native language**: Write down exactly what you want to say in the language you are most comfortable with. This ensures you don't lose any important details.
2.  **Translate to English**: Copy your draft into the translation tool.
3.  **Review the English output**: Read the translated English text. Does it make sense?
    *   *Tip*: You can "reverse translate" (translate the English back to your language) to see if the meaning stayed the same.
4.  **Polish (Optional)**: Paste the English text into **Grammarly** or a similar tool to catch small errors.

### Step 3: Publishing
1.  Copy the final English text.
2.  Paste it into the GitHub comment box or file.
3.  (Optional) It is perfectly okay to add a note like: *"Translated with DeepL - please ask if something is unclear."*
4.  Click **Comment** or **Commit**.

## 4. Best Practices for International English

To make translation easier for everyone (including machines):

*   **Keep sentences short**: Avoid long, complicated sentences with many commas.
*   **Avoid idioms and slang**: Phrases like "it's raining cats and dogs" or "hit the ground running" often translate poorly. Use literal language.
*   **Be explicit**: Instead of "fix it", say "fix the bug in the login function".
*   **Use bullet points**: Lists are often easier to translate and understand than paragraphs.

## 5. Example

**Native Draft (Spanish):**
> "Creo que hay un error en la línea 42. La variable no está definida. Deberíamos inicializarla antes del bucle."

**Translation (English):**
> "I think there is an error on line 42. The variable is not defined. We should initialize it before the loop."
